# Review memo — corrections to `foundations.md` and `systems_recovery.md`

**2026-10-19.** I read both memos and checked the flagged claims against primary documentation. I did not reproduce any benchmark; retained numbers remain source claims.

## foundations.md

**1. Shannon 1951 as “the origin” (§2.2).** Overclaim: n-gram language modeling and next-symbol prediction predate 1951, and Shannon’s 1948 paper already discusses n-gram approximations. **Replace with:** “Shannon (1951) is a foundational milestone in next-symbol prediction and entropy estimation, not the origin of sequence modeling. It is the canonical early demonstration that prediction can estimate the entropy and redundancy of English.” Sources: [Shannon 1951](https://www.princeton.edu/~wbialek/rome/refs/shannon_51.pdf); [Shannon 1948](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf).

**2. `P ≈ 12Ld² + Vd` is presented as general (§7.2).** It is the MHA plus non-gated 4d-MLP special case. With `g` KV heads, attention is `2d² + 2g d²/h`; a gated MLP has three matrices. **Replace with:** “Per layer: attention = `2d² + 2g d²/h`; MLP = `m·d·d_ff` (`m=2` non-gated, `m=3` gated SwiGLU). Thus `P ≈ L(2d² + 2g d²/h + m·d·d_ff) + Vd` for tied embeddings (`2Vd` untied). The `12Ld²` form holds only for MHA (`g=h`) with a non-gated 4d MLP, or a gated MLP with `d_ff=8d/3`; it does not cover GQA/MQA, wide gated MLPs, MLA, or MoE, where total and active parameters must be reported separately.” Sources: [GQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13245); [DeepSeek-V2/MLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04434); [GLU variants](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05202).

**3. “2P FLOPs are amortized over B … intensity rises roughly as 2B/b” (§7.4).** FLOPs are not amortized; they grow with `B`, as does KV traffic. **Replace with:** “Weight bytes are amortized over `B`; FLOPs and KV bytes are not. Per decode step, FLOPs ≈ `B(2P+4Lnd)` and bytes ≈ `b(P+2BLngd_h)` plus activations, so intensity ≈ `B(2P+4Lnd)/[b(P+2BLngd_h)]`. It rises with `B` only while weight traffic dominates and asymptotes to `(2P+4Lnd)/(2Lngd_h b)`, which can remain below the ridge at long `n`. Batching raises utilization; it does not by itself make decode compute-bound.”

**4. `M_state = L·d_inner·N` omits conv state (§7.3).** **Replace with:** “`M_state ≈ L[d_inner·N + d_inner·(d_conv−1)]` elements for Mamba-1, independent of `n`; the second term is the causal-conv buffer (`d_conv=4` in Mamba-1; confirm per config). For Mamba-2, use `L[n_heads·d_head·N + d_inner·(d_conv−1)]`. Weights and small scalars are excluded.” Source: [Mamba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752).

**5. The 4,096 FLOP/byte prefill intensity is treated as proof of compute-bound prefill (§7.4).** It counts only weights and KV bytes. **Replace with:** “This is an illustrative upper bound on prefill intensity, not sufficient evidence of compute-bound prefill. A credible roofline must add activation and intermediate traffic—at minimum `[B,T,d]` reads/writes and the `d_ff` intermediate, plus attention-score traffic when unfused—which can be comparable to or larger than weight bytes at `T=4096`. State the conclusion conditionally: ‘at this `T`, batch, and fusion level, prefill is expected to be compute-bound; measured intensity/MFU is required to confirm it.’” Sources: [Roofline](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~kubitron/cs252/handouts/papers/RooflineVyNoYellow.pdf); [FlashAttention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135).

## systems_recovery.md

**6. “Best prefix reuse” (SGLang row).** No cited head-to-head supports a ranking. **Replace with:** “Strong radix-tree prefix reuse and cache-aware scheduling for multi-turn/agentic/RAG; no independent head-to-head establishes it as best. vLLM also provides prefix sharing/automatic prefix caching, and the better choice depends on routing affinity, cache tiering, and workload.” Sources: [SGLang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07104); [vLLM/PagedAttention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06180).

**7. “Single-node” (llama.cpp row).** False as an absolute: llama.cpp has an RPC backend for remote ggml devices. **Replace with:** “Primarily single-host, with an optional RPC backend that exposes remote ggml devices over TCP or RDMA and supports distributed inference across hosts; the RPC backend is proof-of-concept and insecure on untrusted networks. It still lacks a PagedAttention block table, cluster scheduler, and production PD disaggregation.” Source: [llama.cpp RPC README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/rpc/README.md).

**8. “Compiled engines” as TensorRT-LLM’s only backend.** TensorRT-LLM also has a PyTorch-native backend (beta, v0.17+). **Replace with:** “Dual execution backend: TensorRT compiled engines and a PyTorch-native backend (beta, v0.17+), plus in-flight batching, paged KV, chunked context, FP8/FP4, XQA, and NIXL disaggregation.” Source: [TensorRT-LLM PyTorch Backend](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-LLM/torch.html).

**9. “Disaggregation DOES NOT improve throughput.”** True only as vLLM’s implementation-specific statement about raw throughput, not a universal verdict. **Replace with:** “vLLM’s documentation states that disaggregated prefill does not improve raw throughput in its implementation and positions it for TTFT/ITL isolation and tail control. Disaggregation can raise SLO-constrained goodput or effective capacity when TTFT and TPOT conflict and KV transfer is cheap (DistServe, Mooncake); it can also lose. Report raw throughput and SLO-goodput separately, with GPU count, attainment, workload, and transfer cost.” Sources: [vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/disagg_prefill/); [DistServe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09670); [Mooncake](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast25-qin.pdf).

**10. “CP only for long prefill.”** Decode CP exists. **Replace with:** “CP is primarily a long-prefill TTFT tool, but decode CP (DCP) is supported in vLLM and shards KV along sequence during decode, raising concurrency when KV replication is the limit. It adds per-token communication and is not a universal win. Use CP for long prefill; use DCP only when decode KV capacity, not compute, is binding.” Sources: [vLLM DCP blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-08-07-decode-context-parallelism); [vLLM CP docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/serving/context_parallel_deployment/); [CP paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.01783).

**11. “Every MLA rank holds identical KV.”** True under pure TP, not universally. **Replace with:** “Under pure TP, MLA’s single latent KV head is replicated on every TP rank, so write-back must deduplicate or account for replication. This is layout-dependent: DP attention partitions requests across ranks and eliminates KV duplication; DCP shards the latent by sequence; TPLA shards it by feature dimension. Branch on the actual parallelism layout rather than assuming replication.” Sources: [vLLM DCP blog](https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-08-07-decode-context-parallelism); [TPLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15881); [SGLang DP attention](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2025-05-05-large-scale-ep/).

**12. “NVLink or ≥100 Gbps RDMA.”** Not a universal threshold. **Replace with:** “NVLink, or inter-node RDMA whose measured KV-transfer time is small relative to prefill and TPOT for the target model, TP/PP layout, and SLO. Mooncake’s ≥100 Gbps applies to its global KV-cache deployment; DistServe evaluated a 25 Gbps cross-node testbed; vLLM sets no fixed number. Measure transfer time rather than gating on link rate.” Sources: [Mooncake](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast25-qin.pdf); [DistServe](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.09670); [vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/disagg_prefill/).

**13. Other high-impact correction — vLLM V1 “GPU↔CPU KV swapping was removed” (§2).** Too absolute: vLLM documents an `OffloadingConnector` for CPU KV offload, even though default preemption is recompute-oriented. **Replace with:** “V1’s default preemption is recompute-oriented, but vLLM documents explicit CPU KV offloading via `OffloadingConnector`; distinguish offloading from scheduler swap and scope the claim to default behavior.” Source: [vLLM docs](https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/disagg_prefill/).

## Benchmark claims missing workload or hardware

Every retained number needs model/version, hardware SKU and count, parallelism, ISL/OSL or trace, arrival process/rate, concurrency/batch, SLO target and attainment, comparator version/config, and metric. At least one is missing for: Orca 36.9× (no arrival rate/ISL/OSL/SLO); vLLM 2–4× (no model sizes/hardware/rate/SLO); SGLang 6.4× and Chatbot Arena hit rates (no model/rate/trace/cache size/window); Sarathi 2.6–5.6× (no ISL/OSL/rate/exact SLOs); DistServe 7.4×/12.6× (no ISL/OSL/absolute rate); Mooncake 59–498% and 115%/107% (proprietary trace; no ISL/OSL/SLO/hit rate; define the two percentages); AMPD 67–340% (no trace/SLO targets/baseline configs); FlashInfer 29–69% (no ISL/OSL/concurrency/trace); PagedAttention 20–26% kernel slowdown (no shape/head dim/GPU); vLLM V1 “near-zero overhead at 0% hit rate” (no benchmark setup). Demote these to “reported under the source’s stated setup” and do not rank engines from them. Also label FA4 (arXiv 2603.05451) and AMPD (2602.14516) as unverified 2026 preprints; I did not reproduce them.
